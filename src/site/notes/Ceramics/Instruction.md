## **Session 1: Clay Prep & Centering**

**Objectives:**

- Learn about clay and safety
- Try wedging clay
- Try centering clay on the wheel

**Script:**  
"Fold clay over itself to wedge it. Push down and forward with your palms, repeating until the clay feels smooth. Wedging removes air pockets and makes the clay even, so it’s easier to work with.

Sit close to the wheel, keep your elbows on your knees, and your back straight. Place clay in the center of the wheel head, wet your hands, and press the clay inward with your left hand and downward with your right palm. Centering helps you pull walls evenly and get a smooth, even rim."

---

## **Session 2: Opening & Making the Base**

**Objectives:**

- Open up the clay
- Make a flat base

**Script:**  
"Press your thumbs gently into the center of the clay while the wheel spins. Stop before you reach the wheel head. This makes an opening for your pot.

Pull your thumbs outward to widen the hole, keeping your fingers level. This creates a base for your pot, use your thumb to flatten the base."

---

## **Session 3: Pulling Up the Walls**

**Objectives:**

- Try pulling the walls up
- See how the clay moves

**Script:**  
"Place your left hand inside the pot and your right hand outside. Gently pinch the clay between your fingers and slowly lift upward and inward as the wheel turns. Move your hands together, keeping steady pressure."

---

## **Session 4: Making a Cylinder**

**Objectives:**

- Practice shaping a cylinder
- Learn to check for evenness

**Script:**  
"After pulling up the walls, use both hands to guide the sides straight up. Use your fingers to gently compress the rim."

---
## **Session 5: Making a Bowl**

**Objectives:**

- Try shaping a bowl
- Notice the difference from a cylinder

**Script:**  
"Start like you’re making a cylinder, then gently push outwards with your inside hand as you pull up. Use your outside hand to support the shape. Opening the shape outwards creates a bowl and helps you learn to control curves."

---

## **Session 6: Trimming & Foot Rings**

**Objectives:**

- Try trimming a pot
- Make a simple foot ring

**Script:**  
"Place your leather-hard pot upside down on the wheel and secure it. Use a trimming tool to gently shave away excess clay from the base. Carve a shallow ring around the bottom. Trimming removes extra clay and makes the bottom neat, while a foot ring helps your pot sit evenly and look finished."

---

## **Session 7: Glazing Basics**

**Objectives:**

- Learn how to glaze
- Try dipping or brushing glaze

**Script:**  
"Dip your pot into glaze for about three seconds, then lift and let the extra glaze drip off. Wipe the bottom clean with a sponge. Glazing adds color and a smooth surface to your pot, and cleaning the bottom keeps it from sticking to the kiln shelf."

---

## **Session 8: Review & Practice**

**Objectives:**

- Practice what you’ve learned
- Ask questions and get feedback

**Script:**  
"Choose to make a cup, bowl, or cylinder using the steps you’ve learned. Focus on each stage: centering, opening, pulling, and shaping. Practicing all the steps together helps you remember the process and see how much you’ve improved."

---

**Remember:**

- Encourage everyone to ask questions
- Remind students that it’s okay to make mistakes
- Celebrate effort and progress, not perfection
- Keep instructions short and positive
